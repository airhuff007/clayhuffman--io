Everything I know is in my head.

The systems I've built, the lessons from failed projects, the frameworks I use to make decisions — all of it lives between my ears, and nowhere else. That's a terrible place to store anything important.

I'm not a natural writer. I don't think in essays. I think in systems, in processes, in cause-and-effect chains. But I've noticed something: the people who build the most interesting things are usually the people who can explain what they're building and why.

Not to impress anyone. To think clearly.

## Writing is thinking

Every time I've tried to explain a system out loud — to Lauren, to a client, to anyone who'd listen — I realized partway through that I didn't actually understand it as well as I thought. The gaps show up fast when you have to say the thing out loud. Writing does the same thing.

I started this because I needed to externalize my thinking. Notes in Obsidian helped, but they stay private. There's something different about writing something you know other people might read. It forces precision.

## What I'll write about

Not a niche blog. I don't believe in that for personal sites. I'll write about:

- The builds — what I'm working on, what broke, what shipped
- Health and habits — breathwork, energy, what's actually worked
- Tech — the AI systems I'm building and using
- Whatever I'm thinking about hard enough to write down

If something's worth knowing, it's worth writing. The alternative is keeping it locked in a single skull, and that doesn't help anyone — including me.
