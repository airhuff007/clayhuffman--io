There's a common misconception about the Amish: that they reject technology.

They don't. They evaluate it.

Before a new technology gets adopted into an Amish community, the elders ask a simple question: will this strengthen the community, or will it pull it apart? Televisions got rejected. Power tools got a longer conversation. Phones are complicated — some communities allow them but keep them in a shared building, not in the home.

The point isn't "technology bad." The point is that technology should earn its place. You adopt it because you decided to, not because it arrived and you never chose otherwise.

---

Most of us never made a choice about our relationship with our phones. The phone arrived. We picked it up. And then it was just there, always.

I've been thinking about this because I've noticed a pattern in the people I respect most — the ones building things that last. They're not the ones who are most connected. They're the ones who are most deliberate about when they connect and when they don't.

They have opinions about their tools. They've thought about it.

## The actual question

It's not "should I use this?" It's "is this serving me, or am I serving it?"

Social media is useful. I use it to distribute work and stay connected to what's happening. But I don't have it on my phone's home screen. I check it when I decide to check it. That's a small thing, but it changes the relationship entirely.

## What disconnecting actually gives you

Not peace and quiet — though that too.

The main thing is: long blocks of uninterrupted thought. The kind where you actually solve the problem instead of circling it. The kind where the idea that seemed stuck for a week suddenly arrives on a Tuesday morning because you gave it room.

You can't build something real in 10-minute fragments. The deep work requires depth. The Amish figured this out before the attention economy was invented. They just had better words for it: dwelling, presence, craft.

I'm not going off-grid. But I'm being more deliberate. Every tool earns its place or it doesn't stay.
